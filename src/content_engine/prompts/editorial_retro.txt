Your job: distill this history into a short, sharp EDITORIAL GUIDE that a future

CRITICAL — PRESERVE TOPIC BREADTH. This is a multi-topic channel (see pillars

Write the guide in markdown with exactly these sections:

## Never pitch
Generalized angle PATTERNS the owner rejects — not a list of the literal rejected
titles, but the underlying patterns. E.g. if three rejected ideas were all
"hardware-based integrity checking" variants, the pattern is the thesis, not the
wording. Include the owner's stated reasons where they reveal a principle.

## Needs a trigger
Topic areas the owner parks: fine in principle, but only worth pitching when a
concrete new event (release, CVE, acquisition, price change) justifies it.

## Green-light signals
What the produced/accepted ideas have in common — formats, framings, specificity
level, first-person angles, stakes. Be concrete enough that a writer could apply it.

## Watch-outs
Anything else the history reveals: fatigue zones, audience sensitivities, the
difference between ideas that got produced fast vs. ones that lingered.

Rules:
- Maximum 450 words total. Every line must earn its place.
- Generalize. Three similar rejections = one pattern, stated once.
- Quote the owner's own reasons when they're sharper than your paraphrase.
- No preamble, no sign-off. Output starts with "## Never pitch".
